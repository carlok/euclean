## Why this gate exists

An earlier attempt at a control was built without one. It had fewer
relations and fewer axioms than the theory under test, so it could not
have shown whether that theory is too poor in either direction, and three
further blockers were each independently fatal. The cost of discovering
that afterwards was a sprint; the cost of measuring it first is minutes.

## Axiom liveness

An axiom that is neither a rule nor deliberately seeded contributes
nothing however long the search runs, and the only symptom is a slightly
smaller corpus — indistinguishable from seed variance. That is what the
previous candidate's identity axiom did.

Contributing nothing *at this budget* is a different thing and is not a
failure: the baseline has three such axioms, and a rule that failed the
baseline would be the wrong rule.

- candidate: inert none; idle at this budget none
- baseline: inert none; idle at this budget ['a0', 'a4', 'a9']

## Cost

Probe runtime: candidate 1799.1s, baseline 5.4s, at the same budget.

The candidate costs roughly 333x the baseline for the same
budget. That is a property of the theory rather than of the probe,
and it belongs in the record: whatever this candidate is measured
on later, the grid it would need is that much more expensive.

## What this does not establish

The probe never invokes the proof checker. It measures what the search
reaches, not what is true, and no statement it derives is claimed. An
admitted candidate has its corpus kernel-verified in the build that
follows, like everything else in this project.
